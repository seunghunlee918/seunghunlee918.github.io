**Student Supervision and Letters**

<span style="font-size:13pt;">**Before Asking**</span><br>
<span style="font-size:12pt;">Whether you are asking me to be your advisor or to write a recommendation letter, I expect that you have taken at least one of my courses, worked with me on research (as a co-author or research assistant), or met with me on a few occasions to discuss your research interests.</span><br>


<span style="font-size:13pt;">**Advising**</span><br>
<span style="font-size:12pt;">I supervise students working on topics related to development economics, public economics, and political economy, and I am open to other topics in other areas of applied economics, broadly defined. I am interested in empirical projects using quasi-experimental methods or field and survey experiments. </span><br>

<span style="font-size:12pt;">If I agree to advise you, I expect you to meet with me regularly to present your progress, either one-on-one or in group meetings when I advise several students, and to keep to the milestones we agree upon. </span><br>


<span style="font-size:13pt;">**Recommendation Letters**</span><br>
<span style="font-size:12pt;">I am happy to write letters to support your case for academic conferences, graduate programs, and job applications. Please ask at least **4 weeks** before your deadline. In order to write a detailed letter, I may ask you to provide additional materials.  </span><br>

<span style="font-size:12pt;">I can write the strongest letters when I can speak in detail about your abilities without reservation. Thus, I give priority to students I have advised over a substantial period. Requests from other students are considered case by case. </span><br>

**Resources for Students**
 
 

<span style="font-size:12pt;">It takes a village to become a scholar. These resources come from the people who helped me along the way. The list below will be continuously updated as I find helpful information. Also, please do not hesitate to let me know if there are dead links. </span><br>


<span style="font-size:13pt;">**Research Mindset**</span><br>

- Start by asking "Assuming that I had no data, financial, and ethics constraints, what is the one research question that I want to study?" This helps define who you are as a researcher and identify new projects (I thank Suresh Naidu for asking me this question on our first meeting).
- Tatyana Deryugina has [published an e-book](https://deryugina.com){:target="_blank"} on how to train yourself as an economist at different stages of the career.  
- [Questions you should be asking yourselves](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1yeSwp1KFNZFaKC86VBUgE6CbyyWsc11a/view){:target="_blank"} as you write a paper and prepare your presentation from Donald Davis.
- I am beginning to learn that even as an empiricist, starting from theoretical models help. Here is the lecture note from Patrick François on [connecting empirics to theory](https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2026-09/BREAD%20Week%203%20lecture%20slides.pdf){:target="_blank"} from BREAD-IGC virtual PhD-level course on political economy 2026.
- In social sciences, the paper does not have to be perfect. In fact, Joel Mokyr once said that ["perfect is the enemy of the very good"](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/qaA9lCzXQew){:target="_blank"}.

<span style="font-size:13pt;">**Coding and Other Technical Skills**</span><br>

- You will be using various projects, such as Stata, R, Python, and GIS programs as you embark on an empirical project. You will inevitably run into a trial and error phase that you need to overcome to become proficient at these tools. 
- Work with a good text editor like [VSCode](https://code.visualstudio.com){:target="_blank"} or [Sublime Text](https://www.sublimetext.com){:target="_blank"} to write the code and LaTeX codes.
- Use a [version control](https://michaelstepner.com/blog/git-vs-dropbox/){:target="_blank"} platform like [GitHub](https://github.com){:target="_blank"}.
- [Principles of writing a good code](https://web.stanford.edu/~gentzkow/research/CodeAndData.pdf){:target="_blank"} from Matthew Gentzkow and Jesse Shapiro

<span style="font-size:13pt;">**Writing**</span><br>

- Writing is one of the most difficult challenges as a researcher. To make this life a bit simpler, here are some guides from [Claudia Goldin and Lawrence Katz](https://www.economics.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum5991/files/econ/files/tenruleswriting.pdf){:target="_blank"}, and [John Cochrane](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5e6033a4ea02d801f37e15bb/t/5eda74919c44fa5f87452697/1591374993570/phd_paper_writing.pdf){:target="_blank"}. 
- One book that I still keep with me to this day is [The Little Book of Research Writing](https://www.econscribe.org/textbook){:target="_blank"} by Varanya Chaubey.
- Another useful book on academic writing is [The Craft of Research](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/C/bo215874008.html){:target="_blank"}, recommended by my advisor Rodrigo Soares.
- My writing style is also influenced somewhat by [The Elements of Style](https://www.amazon.com/-/ko/dp/0205313426/ref=mes-dp?_encoding=UTF8&pd_rd_w=FuOkU&content-id=amzn1.sym.554d6923-441d-464e-b555-abe1a6d1f059&pf_rd_p=554d6923-441d-464e-b555-abe1a6d1f059&pf_rd_r=6ZJV45AY2GK8J0VTZAMG&pd_rd_wg=jEtU0&pd_rd_r=f33e382d-0276-4913-a155-cc21be22d8ae){:target="_blank"} by William Strunk Jr. and E.B. White.
- Use reference manager like [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org){:target="_blank"} to save time and effort in maintaining your references. 

<span style="font-size:13pt;">**Presentation**</span><br>

- I generally recommend presenting at external seminars or conferences to get feedback from different angles. You will learn a lot and it helps extend your paper significantly. 
- In terms of organizing your talk and slides, refer to Kim Ruhl's notes on [making robust slides](https://www.kimjruhl.com/presentation-notes){:target="_blank"}.
- Jesse Shapiro has a graphic guide on [how to give applied microeconomics presentations](https://shapiro.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum7731/files/shapiro/files/applied_micro_slides.pdf){:target="_blank"}.
- Academic presentations are done with Beamer (LaTeX) slides. Here is a [comprehensive guide](https://github.com/paulgp/beamer-tips){:target="_blank"} from Paul Goldsmith Pinkham.
- Rafe Meager has slides on [public speaking for academic economists](https://www.dropbox.com/scl/fi/vooa5q6ejnia6aoma0530/public_speaking_for_academic_economists.pdf?rlkey=311am9b8fmoy6ac82txxango5&e=1&dl=0){:target="_blank"}.
